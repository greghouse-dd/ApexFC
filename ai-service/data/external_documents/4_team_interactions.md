# Team Phase Interactions & Matchups

## Build Up vs High Press

### Definition
The tactical duel between a team attempting to progress the ball from their own third (Build-up) and an opponent attempting to recover it immediately near the penalty box (High Press).

### Objective
For the build-up team: to bypass the high press and exploit the massive space left behind it. For the pressing team: to force a deep turnover or a long clearance.

### Key Principles
- Create numerical superiority (+1) using the goalkeeper.
- Attract the press to open vertical passing lanes behind it.
- Maintain short, high-composure passing networks.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Act as a sweeping distributor, acting as the free man.
- **Centre-backs**: Split wide to force opposing forwards to cover larger horizontal distances.
- **Defensive Midfielders**: Drop or rotate to open passing corridors, acting as short link players.
- **Wingers / Striker**: Stand high and wide to stretch the opponent vertically.

### Execution
- Goalkeeper plays to the left centre-back.
- The centre-back attracts the opposing winger, then passes back to the goalkeeper.
- Goalkeeper immediately plays a vertical pass to the dropping midfielder, bypassing the first line.

### Advantages
- Bypassing the press leaves the opponent's midfield and defense exposed in large spaces.

### Variations
- 3+2 build-up against a 4-4-2 press; diamond build-up against a single striker.

### Counters
- pressers using cover shadows effectively to cut off central pivots.

### Relationship With
- Build-Up from the Back, High Press, Numerical Superiority.

## Counter Press vs Rest Defence

### Definition
The interaction that occurs during a turnover in the attacking third, where the attacking team attempts to recover the ball instantly (Counter-press) while maintaining their behind-the-ball security structure (Rest Defence).

### Objective
To sustain pressure on the opponent's box, prevent counter-attacks at source, and ensure that if the counter-press fails, the rest defense is positioned to block the outlet.

### Key Principles
- Immediate ball pressure by the nearest players.
- Rest-defense players must scan and mark the opponent's forward outlets.
- Squeeze central lanes.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Attacking Midfielders / Wingers**: Close down the ball carrier instantly (first wave).
- **Defensive Midfielders**: Squeeze central zones, locking passing lanes to target outlets.
- **Defenders (Rest Defence)**: Tighten marking on the opponent's strikers.

### Tactical Scenario
- The team loses the ball near the opponent's box. The wingers immediately counter-press, forcing the defender into a rushed clearance. The defensive midfielder, sitting in a rest-defense position, easily intercepts the clearance.

### Relationship With
- Counter-Pressing, Rest Defence, Dynamic Rest Defence.

## Positional Play vs Low Block

### Definition
The tactical matchup between a team utilizing zone-based possession structures (Positional Play) and an opponent defending deep inside their own third (Low Block).

### Objective
For the possessing team: to break down the deep block through lateral ball circulation, wide switches, and movements in half-spaces. For the defending team: to maintain compactness and clear crosses.

### Key Principles
- Circulate the ball quickly to shift the opponent block horizontally.
- Occupy the half-spaces and Vertical Lanes.
- Create 1v1 wide isolation situations (Overload to Isolate).

### Execution
- Overload the left side.
- Switch the ball to the isolated right winger.
- Right winger cuts back to the arriving midfielder in the box.

### Relationship With
- Positional Play, Low Block, Half-Spaces, Overloads.

## Attacking Transition vs Rest Defence

### Definition
The matchup between a team launching a counter-attack immediately after winning possession (Attacking Transition) and the opponent's defensive structure positioned to prevent it (Rest Defence).

### Objective
For the transition team: to find forward outlets and attack space before the opponent can retreat. For the rest defense: to slow down or intercept the transition.

### Key Principles
- Direct forward passing.
- Lock the opponent's rest-defense players with decoy runs.
- Rest-defense players must delay the attacker's progression (jockeying) to allow teammates to run back.

### Relationship With
- Attacking Transition, Rest Defence.
