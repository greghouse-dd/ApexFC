# Player Roles & Movements

## False Nine

### Definition
A centre-forward who drops away from the opposition's defensive line into midfield areas during possession, vacating the striker position.

### Objective
To pull the opponent's centre-backs out of position, create midfield numerical overloads, and open vertical space for wingers or attacking midfielders to make runs behind the defense.

### Key Principles
- Drop into the space between the opponent's midfield and defensive lines.
- Open body shape to receive and turn forward.
- Coordinate runs: when the False Nine drops, a winger must immediately run behind.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Wingers**: Make diagonal runs from wide areas into the vacant central spaces behind the opponent's defense.
- **Midfielders**: Combine with the False Nine to overload the central zones.
- **Striker (False Nine)**: Drop deep into the "number ten" space, link play, and turn to play vertical passes.

### Team Shape
- **Spacing**: Fluid central spacing, creating a box or diamond midfield.

### Triggers
- Opponent centre-backs marking tightly.
- Midfield space open for receiving.

### Execution
- False Nine drops into midfield.
- Centre-back follows, opening a central gap, or passes are compiled around the free forward.
- Release wingers into the opened space.

### Advantages
- Overloads the midfield.
- Disorganizes the opponent's defensive line.

### Weaknesses
- Lacks a direct box presence for crosses.
- Requires technically elite and highly mobile forwards.

### Risks
- Can lead to attacking congestion if midfielders don't rotate correctly.

### Coaching Points
- "Drop to draw them out, then slide the pass through."

### Best Used Against
- Rigid back-four systems with defenders who mark tightly.

### Relationship With
- Positional Play, Blind-side run, Pinning a defender.

### Elite Examples
- Lionel Messi (under Pep Guardiola at Barcelona), Roberto Firmino (under Klopp at Liverpool).

## Inverted Full-Back

### Definition
A full-back who moves from their traditional wide defensive position into central midfield areas when their team is in possession.

### Objective
To create a numerical overload (often a double pivot) in central midfield, protect against counter-attacks through the center (rest defense), and open wide passing lanes for the wingers.

### Key Principles
- Move central early in build-up.
- Form passing triangles with the centre-backs and pivots.
- Protect central space during turnovers.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Centre-backs**: Split wider to cover the space vacated by the full-back.
- **Wingers**: Stay wide to maintain width on the flank.
- **Full-back (Inverted)**: Tuck inside to act as a central midfielder, assisting in ball circulation and defensive screening.

### Team Shape
- **Formations**: Shifting from a 4-3-3 in defense to a 3-2-4-1 in attack.

### Triggers
- Clean build-up established from the back.
- Opponent pressing with two forwards.

### Advantages
- Secures the center against counter-attacks.
- Creates central passing options.

### Weaknesses
- Exposes wide channels on transition.

### Relationship With
- Rest Defence, Double Pivot.

### Elite Examples
- John Stones, Kyle Walker (Man City), Trent Alexander-Arnold (Liverpool).

## Target Man

### Definition
A physically strong forward who serves as a direct outlet for deep passes, using their height, strength, and hold-up play to retain possession.

### Objective
To bypass opponent pressure instantly, hold the ball under pressure, and lay it off for arriving teammates.

### Key Principles
- Lock the defender with physical contact.
- Play with back to goal.
- Secure the ball before looking to release teammates.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Midfielders**: Push up immediately to support and win second balls.
- **Wingers**: Make forward runs to receive knock-downs.
- **Striker (Target Man)**: Challenge in the air, hold up ball, and shield it.

### Best Used Against
- Teams with high defensive lines and high-pressing units.

### Relationship With
- Direct Play, Second Balls.

### Elite Examples
- Ivan Toney, Olivier Giroud.

---

## Deep-Lying Playmaker

### Definition
A defensive or central midfielder who operates deep in their own half, controlling the game's tempo through passing range and tactical awareness.

### Objective
To dictate possession, orchestrate build-up, and progress the ball cleanly through lines.

### Relationship With
- Build-Up from the Back, Double Pivot.

### Elite Examples
- Andrea Pirlo, Rodri, Toni Kroos.

---

## Libero

### Definition
A defender who operates behind or alongside the main defensive line with the freedom to step into midfield and participate in build-up play.

### Elite Examples
- Franz Beckenbauer, John Stones (in modern interpretations).

---

## Overlap

### Definition
An attacking run where a supporting player sprints around the outside of the teammate in possession, moving toward the flank.

### Relationship With
- Width, Overlapping Run.

---

## Underlap

### Definition
An attacking run where a wide supporting player sprints inside the player in possession, exploiting the gap (half-space) between the opponent's full-back and centre-back.

### Relationship With
- Half-Spaces, Underlapping Run.

---

## Blind-side run

### Definition
An offensive run made by an attacker into the space behind a defender where the defender cannot see both the ball and the runner simultaneously.

### Objective
To penetrate the defensive line without detection, giving the runner a head start to receive a pass.

### Key Principles
- Run behind the defender's shoulder.
- Timing: run when the ball carrier has time to pass.

### Elite Examples
- Mohamed Salah, Raheem Sterling.

---

## Pinning a defender (Pinning)

### Definition
A tactical behavior where an attacking player positions themselves to occupy a defender, preventing that defender from sliding, stepping up, or covering teammates.

### Objective
To lock specific defenders in place, creating space for teammates to receive or run into.

### Tactical Scenario
- The striker stands directly between the two centre-backs. When the winger cuts inside, neither centre-back can step up to challenge him because they are pinned by the threat of the striker running behind.

---

## Decoy runs

### Definition
A run made by an attacker with the deliberate intention of dragging a defender away from a specific space, opening that space for a teammate.

---

## Curved pressing runs

### Definition
A defensive run made by a pressing player that curves outward-to-inward or inward-to-outward, forcing the ball carrier to pass in a single direction while using their cover shadow to block other passing options.

---

## Third-man runs

### Definition
A tactical combination where Player A passes to Player B to attract defensive attention, while Player C (the third man) makes a run into open space to receive a quick lay-off pass from Player B.
