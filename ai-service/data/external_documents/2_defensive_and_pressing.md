# Defensive Structures, Pressing & Rest Defence

## High Press

### Definition
An aggressive defensive strategy where a team applies pressure high up the pitch inside the opponent's defensive third, attempting to disrupt their build-up immediately.

### Objective
To force turnovers close to the opponent's goal, restrict clean passing options, and prevent controlled progression.

### Key Principles
-Squeeze the pitch vertically by maintaining a high defensive line.
-Lock passing lanes using cover shadows.
-Force play toward pressing traps (e.g., locking the ball on the touchline).
-Coordinated movement: when one presses, the entire team shifts to support.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Position high outside the penalty area to act as a sweeper-keeper, cleaning up long balls behind the defensive line.
- **Centre-backs**: Maintain a high defensive line, mark opposing forwards tightly, and prepare for long clearances.
- **Full-backs**: Push up high to press opposing full-backs or slide inside to cover midfielders.
- **Midfielders**: Lock down opposing pivots, mark tightly, and intercept short passes.
- **Wingers**: Press the opponent's centre-backs while blocking passing lanes to the full-backs using their cover shadows.
- **Striker**: Initiate the press by angling their run to force the goalkeeper or centre-back to pass in a specific direction.

### Team Shape
- **Formations**: 4-3-3 or 4-4-2 pressing shapes.
- **Spacing**: Stretched vertically but highly compact horizontally (within 25–30 yards from front to back).
- **Distances**: Very close distances (5–8 yards) between lines to deny space.

### Triggers
- Goalkeeper plays a slow pass to the centre-back.
- Opponent centre-back receives with their back to the pitch.
- A weak or inaccurate pass.
- Ball played to the full-back near the touchline.

### Execution
- Striker curves their run to block the switch of play.
- Far winger slides narrow to block central lanes.
- Near winger presses the receiving centre-back.
- Midfielders step up to lock the short passing options.
- The ball carrier is forced into a rushed clearance or a turnover.

### Advantages
- Forces turnovers near the opponent's goal, creating immediate scoring opportunities.
- Prevents the opponent from establishing control.

### Weaknesses
- Exposes space behind the high defensive line.
- Extremely vulnerable if the press is uncoordinated or bypassed.

### Risks
- Fast opponents can exploit the space behind with long vertical passes.

### Common Mistakes
- Individual players pressing in isolation (creates gaps).
- Defensive line dropping deep while forwards press high (stretches the team).

### Coaching Points
- "Press as a unit."
- "Lock the touchline."
- "Use your cover shadow."

### Training Exercises
- 6v6 build-up vs pressing drills in a restricted double-box zone.

### Variations
- Man-oriented high press, zonal high press, jump-pressing from midfield.

### Counters
- Direct direct play (long balls to a target man).
- Third-man combinations using deep drop-off midfielders.

### Best Used Against
- Teams that insist on short build-up from the back but lack technical composure under pressure.

### Relationship With
- Pressing Triggers, Cover Shadow, Build-Up from the Back.

### Elite Examples
- Liverpool (Klopp era), Manchester City, Arsenal (Arteta).

### Tactical Scenario
- The opponent's goalkeeper plays a pass to the left centre-back. The striker immediately angles his run to block the pass back to the goalkeeper. The right winger steps up, using his cover shadow to block the line to the left-back, forcing the centre-back into a rushed pass that is intercepted by the central midfielder.

## Mid-Block

### Definition
A defensive structure where a team organizes its defensive shape primarily in the middle third of the pitch.

### Objective
To deny central space, protect the midfield channels, and wait for specific triggers to apply pressure, avoiding over-commitment high up the pitch.

### Key Principles
- Maintain vertical and horizontal compactness.
- Protect the center, forcing the opponent to play wide.
- Maintain a medium-high defensive line to deny space between the lines.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Position at the edge of the penalty box.
- **Centre-backs**: Maintain line height and direct the midfield shifting.
- **Full-backs**: Stay compact with the centre-backs, sliding wide only when the ball is played to the opposing winger.
- **Midfielders**: Form a compact screen, slide laterally, and deny vertical passing lanes.
- **Wingers**: Drop to form a second bank of four, protecting the half-spaces.
- **Striker**: Position near the center circle to block passes to the opposing pivot.

### Team Shape
- **Formations**: 4-4-2 or 4-5-1 compact blocks.
- **Spacing**: 8–10 yards between lines.
- **Distances**: Compacting the shape to 20 yards vertically.

### Triggers
- Opponent midfielder receives facing own goal.
- Pass played sideways or backward.
- Opponent makes a technical error.

### Execution
- Team shifts laterally as the ball moves.
- When a trigger is met, the nearest player presses, and the block squeezes.
- Secure the ball and counter-attack.

### Advantages
- Highly balanced; minimizes space behind while denying central progression.
- Conserves player energy.

### Weaknesses
- Allows the opponent's centre-backs time and possession on the ball.
- Can drop too deep if pressure is not applied when triggers occur.

### Risks
- Midfield fatigue from constant lateral sliding.

### Common Mistakes
- Midfielders stepping out of the block individually, breaking compactness.
- Wingers failing to drop, exposing the full-backs.

### Coaching Points
- "Shift together."
- "Force wide."
- "Deny the center."

### Training Exercises
- 8v8 shifting drills focusing on maintaining distances relative to a moving ball.

### Variations
- Squeezing mid-block, passive mid-block.

### Counters
- Switch of play to isolate wide players.
- Inverting full-backs to create central overloads.

### Best Used Against
- High-possession teams that look to build through the center.

### Relationship With
- Low Block, Compactness.

### Elite Examples
- Atletico Madrid (Simeone), Villarreal (Emery).

### Tactical Scenario
- The opponent's centre-back circulates the ball laterally. The defending 4-4-2 mid-block slides across in unison, keeping passing lanes through the center completely closed, eventually forcing a sideways pass that initiates the pressing trigger.

## Low Block

### Definition
A deep, compact defensive structure where the defending team organizes their entire block inside or just outside their own penalty area.

### Objective
To deny all space in behind, protect the box, and prevent the opponent from creating high-quality scoring chances, relying on quick counter-attacks upon recovery.

### Key Principles
- Extreme horizontal and vertical compactness.
- Protect the center and the box at all costs.
- Aggressive body-blocking of shots and crosses.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Dominate the box on crosses, command the defensive line.
- **Centre-backs**: Command the defensive line, defend the box, win aerial duels, block shots.
- **Full-backs**: Stay narrow to protect the post, block wide crosses.
- **Midfielders**: Sit deep screen, cover the space in front of the box, track runners from deep.
- **Wingers**: Drop deep to assist full-backs, forming a bank of five or six when necessary.
- **Striker**: Stay high to offer an outlet for counter-attacks.

### Team Shape
- **Formations**: 5-4-1 or 4-5-1 very deep blocks.
- **Spacing**: 5–8 yards between players.
- **Distances**: Squeezing the block vertically to 15 yards.

### Triggers
- Possession lost in own half.
- Lead secured late in a match.
- Opponent overloading the final third.

### Execution
- Force the opponent to wide areas.
- Block the cross or intercept the pass inside the box.
- Play a direct clearance to the outlet striker to launch a counter-attack.

### Advantages
- Removes the risk of space in behind.
- Makes it very difficult for the opponent to create clear-cut central chances.

### Weaknesses
- Invites sustained pressure.
- Defending close to own goal increases the risk of deflections, penalties, or long-range shots.

### Risks
- Conceding set-pieces and second balls around the box.

### Common Mistakes
- Defenders dropping too deep too early, playing opponents onside.
- Midfielders failing to track late runs from deep.

### Coaching Points
- "Defend the width of the box."
- "Block the shot."
- "Stay compact."

### Training Exercises
- Attack vs Defense (10v8) focusing on low-block defensive clearances and blocks.

### Variations
- Back-five low block, narrow back-four low block.

### Counters
- Underlapping runs, rapid switches, cutbacks, long-range shooting.

### Best Used Against
- Physically dominant or fast teams that exploit space behind.

### Relationship With
- Counter-Attacking, Mid-Block.

### Elite Examples
- Chelsea (under Mourinho or Tuchel in specific Champions League matches).

### Tactical Scenario
- The attacking team circulates the ball around the box. The defending team remains inside the box, blocking three consecutive cross attempts and clearing the ball to the striker.

## Counter-Pressing (Gegenpressing)

### Definition
The immediate, aggressive attempt to regain possession of the ball in the transition phase within 5–8 seconds of losing it.

### Objective
To recover the ball quickly before the opponent can organize a counter-attack, capitalizing on their temporary transition disorganization.

### Key Principles
- Attack the ball carrier immediately.
- Close down passing options aggressively.
- Lock nearby options using cover shadows.
- Maintain defensive balance behind the press.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Squeeze up high to cover clearances.
- **Centre-backs**: Step up to mark target outlets and secure the rest defense.
- **Full-backs**: Support the press or slide central to cover.
- **Midfielders**: Apply immediate pressure to the ball carrier or cover passing lanes.
- **Wingers**: Press the ball from the blind side.
- **Striker**: Apply immediate pressure, chasing the ball carrier.

### Team Shape
- **Spacing**: High density of players around the ball zone.
- **Distances**: Very close distances to ensure immediate pressure.

### Triggers
- Turnover of possession in the final or middle third.
- Opponent ball carrier facing backward or trapped near the touchline.

### Execution
- The ball is lost.
- The 3 nearest players sprint toward the ball carrier.
- Remaining players lock down passing lanes.
- Possession is recovered, and the team resumes attacking.

### Advantages
- Sustains attacking pressure.
- Prevents counter-attacks at source.
- Catches the opponent disorganized.

### Weaknesses
- High physical demand.
- Leaving space behind if the counter-press is bypassed.

### Risks
- If the first wave of pressure is beaten, the opponent has space to run at the backline.

### Common Mistakes
- Half-hearted pressing (allows the opponent time to pass).
- Forgetting the rest defense behind the press.

### Coaching Points
- "React in the first 3 seconds."
- "Squeeze the ball."
- "Lock the options."

### Training Exercises
- 4v4+2 transition rondos focusing on counter-pressing immediately upon losing the ball.

### Variations
- Ball-oriented counter-pressing, space-oriented counter-pressing, passing lane-oriented counter-pressing.

### Counters
- Quick, one-touch passing to release the ball from the pressure zone.
- Direct long ball to the opposite flank.

### Best Used Against
- Teams that play slow, short passes inside their own half.

### Relationship With
- Attacking Transition, Rest Defence, Pressing Triggers.

### Elite Examples
- Liverpool under Jürgen Klopp, Borussia Dortmund.

### Tactical Scenario
- The team loses possession in the attacking third. The three nearest attackers sprint to close down the opponent who won the ball, forcing him into a loose pass that is intercepted by the attacking midfielder.

## Rest Defence

### Definition
Rest Defence (Restverteidigung) refers to the positioning of defensive players who remain prepared to defend while their team is in possession of the ball.

### Objective
To control and stop potential opponent counter-attacks before they start, ensuring defensive stability during turnovers.

### Key Principles
- Maintain numerical superiority over the opponent's forwards.
- Occupy central lanes to block direct counter-attacking pathways.
- Squeeze up vertically as the team attacks to remain compact.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Goalkeeper**: Maintain high positioning to clean up clearances.
- **Centre-backs**: Position to mark the opposing striker and secure the rest defense.
- **Full-backs**: Tuck inside to form a back three or sit in double-pivot zones to prevent counters.
- **Midfielders**: Remain behind the ball, covering central zones.
- **Wingers**: Maintain wide positions (not directly involved in rest defense).
- **Striker**: Stay high to pin defenders (not directly involved).

### Team Shape
- **Formations**: 3+2 or 2+3 structures behind the ball.
- **Spacing**: Compact defensive balance behind the attacking line.

### Triggers
- Team progresses into the middle and attacking thirds.
- Possession secured in advanced areas.

### Execution
- As attackers advance, the defensive midfielders and defenders form a shield behind the ball.
- When possession is lost, this shield immediately intercepts or slows the counter.

### Advantages
- Prevents counter-attacks.
- Sustains attacking pressure.

### Weaknesses
- Reduces the number of players committed to the attack.

### Risks
- Being caught flat-footed if defenders fail to read transition moments.

### Common Mistakes
- Defenders watching the attack instead of positioning relative to the opponent's strikers.
- Midfielders committing forward too early.

### Coaching Points
- "Defend while attacking."
- "Secure the center."

### Training Exercises
- 6v4 + 2 transition drills focusing on the defensive shield.

### Variations
- Back-three rest defense, double-pivot rest defense.

### Counters
- Isolated pace wingers running wide channels.

### Best Used Against
- Highly vertical counter-attacking teams.

### Relationship With
- Counter-Attacking, Dynamic Rest Defence, Counter-Pressing.

### Elite Examples
- Manchester City under Guardiola (using inverted full-backs to secure the center).

### Tactical Scenario
- The team attacks down the left flank. The right-back tucks in next to the defensive midfielder, creating a 3+2 structure behind the ball. When the attack is cleared, the right-back immediately intercepts the clearance, sustaining the attack.

## Dynamic Rest Defence

### Definition
Dynamic Rest Defence is the continuous adjustment of defensive structures and player positioning while in possession, responding to the opponent's movements and potential transition threats in real time.

### Objective
To maintain optimal defensive balance against fluid, moving counter-attacking structures.

### Key Principles
- Continuous scanning of the opponent's outlets.
- Adjust positioning as the ball progresses laterally or vertically.
- Seamless communication between defensive units.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Centre-backs**: Shift laterally, adjusting distance to opponent's forwards.
- **Defensive Midfielders**: Squeeze central zones, keeping cover shadows over outlet pathways.
- **Full-backs**: Invert or retreat based on transition triggers.

### Team Shape
- **Spacing**: Adapts dynamically, shifting from 3+2 to 2+4 or 3+1 based on ball position.

### Triggers
- Ball shifts from left to right wing.
- Opponent striker moves to create a counter-attacking channel.

### Execution
- As the ball moves, the rest-defense unit moves in synchronization, locking the passing lanes to the opponent's forward outlets.

### Advantages
- Highly adaptable; prevents target strikers from finding open spaces.

### Weaknesses
- Highly complex; requires elite communication and physical stamina.

### Risks
- A single player failing to adjust can leave a massive counter-attacking channel open.

### Coaching Points
- "Adjust your distance to the outlet."
- "Do not watch the ball; watch the runner."

### Relationship With
- Rest Defence, Pressing Shadows.

## Touchline Trap

### Definition
A defensive pressing strategy that uses the touchline as an extra defender, locking the opponent on the flank to force a turnover.

### Objective
To restrict the opponent's passing options to 180 degrees, forcing them into a high-pressure zone.

### Key Principles
- Direct play wide.
- Block backward and inward passing lanes.
- Squeeze the ball carrier aggressively against the touchline.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Full-backs**: Step up to close down the opposing winger.
- **Midfielders**: Slide over to block central passing channels.
- **Wingers**: Lock the passing lane back to the centre-back.

### Triggers
- Opponent plays a pass to the full-back or winger near the touchline.

### Execution
- Direct opponent wide.
- As the ball travels, the winger, midfielder, and full-back squeeze, locking all passing lanes.
- Win possession.

### Relationship With
- Pressing Triggers, High Press, Cover Shadow.

## Trap Pressing (Pressing Traps)

### Definition
A tactical setup where the defending team deliberately leaves a passing option open to entice the opponent into a specific zone, before immediately closing down the receiver with multiple players.

### Objective
To force predictable passes into zones where the defending team has established a numerical advantage, leading to turnovers.

### Key Principles
- Leave a target option open.
- Coordinate the trigger.
- Close down the target aggressively.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Midfielders**: Position to leave a passing lane open, then close it once the pass is made.
- **Centre-backs**: Support from behind, ready to contest the receiver.

### Triggers
- Ball played into the designated trap zone.

### Execution
- Leave the opposing midfielder unmarked.
- As the pass is launched, three players converge on him, forcing a turnover.

### Relationship With
- Pressing Triggers, High Press.

---

## Pressing Shadows (Cover Shadows)

### Definition
A defensive technique where a pressing player positions their body to block a passing lane to an opponent behind them, effectively taking that opponent out of the game without marking them directly.

### Objective
To reduce the opponent's passing options while applying pressure to the ball carrier, allowing one player to neutralize two.

### Key Principles
- Maintain correct body angle.
- Scan behind to check the position of the blocked opponent.
- Keep distance close.

### Player Responsibilities
- **Wingers**: Block lanes to full-backs while pressing centre-backs.
- **Midfielders**: Block lanes to pivots while pressing forwards.

### Relationship With
- High Press, Pressing Triggers.
